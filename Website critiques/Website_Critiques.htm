Website Critiques

Pitchfork

http://pitchfork.com/

  1. What was your first impression of this site?
  2. How does this site organize the flow of information? (Do they have links on the left and text on the right? Some other scheme? No scheme?)
  3. Does the site structure make sense or does it confuse you? Why?
  4. Does the site graphics/layout/multimedia enhance the site or detract from it? Why?
  5. How long did it take for the home page to load? Was the load time reasonable?
  6. Was the text color and size readable? If a background color or image was used, did it enhance or detract from the site?
  7. Did the site have a navigation bar or other navigation tool? Was the navigation well done?
  8. Did the site have a color scheme or look and feel that was repeated on all the pages?
  9. How does the site respond to smaller window sizes (responsive design)?
  10. What does this site offer that was unusual or very well done?

My first impression of the site was that I instantly recognized it as being a journalism/news website-- whether if that was what they were going for I'm not sure. The fonts and layout of the different articles on the main page reminds me a lot of old Buzzfeed and it introduced a lot of information from the start, an almost overwhelming amount.

On the left most side there are two to three news titles linking to their articles with the dates included at the bottom. The largest and main section I assume is dedicated to the 'headliners.' Then on the right side there is the the latest articles which du not include cover images, making me assume they are less important. The next section of information is the major reviews, below or is the 'more reviews section,' again, making me assume it is less important. Finally, the section before scrolling down to the footer is the 'More From Pitchfork' section that has miscellaneous articles not organized in any specific way it seems, just in a grid layout.

The structure makes sense to me, but a I feel like it only makes sense to me because I've encountered lots of websites just like it before. To navigate it, none of it confuses me on where I need to go, but it is strange how there is a header section for the different parts of the website, but then has it again in a sub menu right next to it with the three lines.

I think the multimedia parts of it help it a lot, but its restrained to just a grid layout so it makes it feel more like a scrapbook more than a cohesive site that I can scroll through-- it has so much information it is trying to advertise that it becomes overwhelming.

I counted, and it only takes about 2 seconds for the main menu to fully load (images included). I'd consider this to be a well optimized website, especially since the images loaded nearly instantly

Outside of the images and the subscribe button in the corner, the entire website is black and white. It definitely makes the text easy to read, but it doesn't have very much personality because of it. The font size however clearly communicated what text should be read first and what is extra referential information

It did have a three line navigation pop up in the top left-hand corner but it confused me, because it just showed the exact same links as the header section. This could have been done so that it can be accessed even when you scroll away from the top, but it still felt repetitive. I also didn't like how if I clicked on it, the menu would take up my entire screen instead of just being contained to the text it was showing.

The entire color scheme was black and white with very sparse red icons throughout. It followed a grid layout which was easy to navigate, but it felt odd to me that a website dedicated to music had a website that lacked any personality or flare.

The website responds very well to smaller window sizes, I would even argue some of the layout makes more sense when I condensed the width of the window.

I think by far its biggest strength was it categorization outside of the main menu. It always linked to either related information or articles that could take you to a different topic. But that kind of came at the cost of perhaps being too bland. The black and white coloring is not necessarily a bad thing, it was just how much it relied on it that mad it feel unusual.
